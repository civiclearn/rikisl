Residency requirements by category

The time it takes to become an Icelandic citizen depends on your personal circumstances. The Icelandic Citizenship Act (Lög um íslenskan ríkisborgararétt, nr. 100/1952) sets different residency requirements for different categories of applicants.

CategoryResidency required
General applicants7 years of continuous residence
Married to an Icelandic citizen3 years (marriage must have lasted at least 4 years)
Nordic citizens (Denmark, Norway, Sweden, Finland)4 years
Recognised refugees5 years
Former Icelandic citizens1 year

These are minimum residency periods. You must have lived in Iceland continuously during this time — extended absences may reset or pause the clock.

The language requirement

Regardless of which category you fall into, you must pass the ríkisborgarapróf (Icelandic citizenship language test) at CEFR A2 level. There is no exemption based on residency duration alone. The exam tests reading, listening, writing, and speaking — all equally weighted.

The only exception is for applicants aged 65 or older, or those with documented medical conditions that prevent them from learning a language, who may be exempt from the test.

Years 1–5: Living and learning

Most immigrants start learning Icelandic through the publicly subsidised 240-hour basic programme (grunnnám í íslensku). This programme is available at adult education centres (símenntunarstöðvar) throughout the country. The programme's final goals align with the CEFR A2 level — exactly what the ríkisborgarapróf requires.

Year 6: Preparing for the exam

You don't need to wait until year 7 to sit the exam. Many applicants take the ríkisborgarapróf in their 6th year so the result is ready when they become eligible to apply. The exam is held twice a year (spring and autumn), so plan accordingly.

Year 7+: The application

Once you have 7 years of continuous residency and a passing exam result, you can submit your citizenship application through Útlendingastofnun (the Directorate of Immigration). The application fee is 60,000 ISK. Processing typically takes several months.

What can delay your application?

  • Gaps in residency — extended time abroad may mean you need to restart the clock.
  • Criminal record — certain offences can delay or disqualify your application.
  • Financial issues — you must not have received certain types of social assistance in the qualifying period.
  • Missing the exam — the test is only held twice a year with limited spots. If registration fills up, you may need to wait 6 months for the next session.

The parliamentary route

If you don't meet all the standard criteria, you can petition the Althingi (Icelandic parliament) for citizenship. The parliament considers applications twice per year and has discretion to grant citizenship outside the normal requirements. This is not unusual — it's a standard part of the Icelandic system.

Key takeaway: Start preparing for the ríkisborgarapróf well before you reach your residency threshold. The exam is the one requirement entirely within your control — don't let it be the bottleneck.
