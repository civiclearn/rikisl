The Ukrainian community in Iceland

Ukrainians are now the second-largest immigrant group in Iceland, making up about 5.3% of all immigrants. The community has grown rapidly since 2022, when Iceland extended temporary collective protection to people fleeing the war. As of early 2025, this protection has been extended until March 2026.

If you arrived under temporary protection, citizenship may feel like a distant goal — but it's worth understanding the process early so you can prepare when the time comes.

Timeline: from arrival to citizenship eligibility

The 7-year residency requirement

The standard path to Icelandic citizenship requires 7 years of continuous residence. If you arrived in 2022, the earliest you would be eligible to apply is 2029. If you are married to an Icelandic citizen, the requirement is reduced to 3 years. For recognised refugees, it's 5 years.

Time spent under temporary protection generally counts toward the residency requirement, though the legal status of this period may be clarified as the first wave of Ukrainian residents approaches eligibility.

The language requirement starts now

Regardless of when you become eligible to apply, the ríkisborgarapróf is the same for everyone. Starting your Icelandic studies early gives you the biggest advantage. The exam is at CEFR A2 — achievable within 2–3 years of consistent study, especially if you're living and working in Iceland.

Advantages for Ukrainian speakers

  • Case system experience — Ukrainian has 7 grammatical cases. Icelandic has only 4. The concept of declining nouns and adjectives will feel natural.
  • Rich consonant inventory — Ukrainian has a complex sound system that prepares you for Icelandic consonant clusters.
  • Strong work ethic and community — the Ukrainian community in Iceland is active and growing, with support networks and social groups in the capital area.

Challenges to prepare for

  • Vocabulary — there is almost no overlap between Ukrainian and Icelandic words. Every word must be learned from scratch.
  • The Latin alphabet — if you're more comfortable with Cyrillic, you'll need to adjust to reading and writing in Latin script. This is usually a quick adaptation, especially if you already know some English.
  • Pronunciation — Icelandic sounds like ð (soft "th"), þ (hard "th"), and the double-L "tl" sound don't exist in Ukrainian.
  • Articles — Icelandic uses a suffixed definite article (hestur = horse, hesturinn = the horse). Ukrainian has no articles, so this concept requires practice.

Free resources for Ukrainian speakers

  • Grunnnám í íslensku — the subsidised 240-hour basic Icelandic programme at adult education centres. Enrol as soon as possible after arriving.
  • Icelandic Online — free university-level course at icelandiconline.com.
  • Tungumálatorg — a free practice platform with exercises organised by topic and level.
  • Múltakraft and other NGOs — several organisations offer free Icelandic classes specifically for Ukrainian newcomers.
  • RÚV — the national broadcaster. Watch news and programmes with subtitles for daily listening practice.
Start early, even if citizenship feels far away. The language test is the one requirement you can work on from day one. The residency clock runs in the background — use that time to build your Icelandic skills.
