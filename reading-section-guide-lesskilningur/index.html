Types of texts, common topics, and strategies for the reading comprehension section of the ríkisborgarapróf.

What the reading section tests

The reading section (lesskilningur) of the ríkisborgarapróf tests your ability to understand simple Icelandic texts at CEFR A2 level. This means you should be able to read and understand:

  • Short, simple texts about everyday topics
  • Notices, signs, and public information
  • Simple personal letters or messages
  • Short newspaper articles on familiar subjects
  • Simple instructions (recipes, directions, guidelines)

You are not expected to read complex texts, literary prose, or technical documents. The focus is on practical, everyday reading comprehension.

Types of texts you may encounter

Based on the exam's A2 level and the curriculum for basic Icelandic (grunnnám), typical reading tasks include:

Text typeExample topics
Notices and signsPublic transport schedules, opening hours, community events
AdvertisementsJob ads, apartment listings, sale announcements
Short articlesLocal news, weather, community stories
Personal messagesEmails, postcards, text messages between friends
Official lettersSimple letters from schools, landlords, or municipal offices
InstructionsSimple recipes, directions, how-to guides

Common question formats

Reading questions typically ask you to:

  • Identify the main idea — what is this text about?
  • Find specific information — when does the event start? How much does it cost? Where is the meeting?
  • Match information — connect people to descriptions, or events to dates.
  • True/false/not stated — determine whether statements match the text.

Essential vocabulary areas

At A2 level, reading texts draw from a core vocabulary of everyday topics. Focus your study on these areas:

  • Family and relationships — fjölskylda, maður/kona, barn, vinur, foreldrar
  • Home and housing — íbúð, herbergi, eldhús, leiga, húsnæði
  • Work and jobs — vinna, starf, laun, verkefni, samstarfsfólk
  • Shopping and money — verð, kaupa, borga, verslun, tilboð
  • Health and body — heilsa, læknir, veikur, lyf, tími (appointment)
  • Transport and places — strætó, bíll, flugvöllur, götu, leiðin
  • Weather and nature — veður, rigning, snjór, vindur, hitastig
  • Time and dates — dagur, vika, mánuður, klukkan, morgun/kvöld

Strategies for the reading section

1. Read the questions first

Before reading the text, scan the questions. This tells you what to look for and prevents you from getting lost in unfamiliar vocabulary.

2. Don't panic over unknown words

You will encounter words you don't know. That's normal at A2 level. Focus on the words you do know and use context to guess the meaning of unfamiliar ones. You don't need to understand every word to answer the questions correctly.

3. Look for cognates and compound words

Icelandic builds words by compounding. If you know "bók" (book) and "safn" (collection), you can guess "bókasafn" = library. This strategy works for many compound nouns.

4. Pay attention to numbers, dates, and names

Factual questions often ask about specific details — prices, times, dates, names, locations. These are easy to spot in a text even if you don't understand every sentence around them.

5. Practise with real Icelandic texts

Read real-world Icelandic content every day: bus schedules, supermarket flyers, short news articles on Vísir.is, public notices at your workplace. The exam uses texts that look and feel like these.

The reading section rewards preparation. Unlike speaking (which requires real-time confidence) or listening (which moves at the speaker's pace), reading lets you go at your own speed. Build your vocabulary steadily, practise with real texts, and this section becomes one of the easiest to score well on.
