The best free tools for building A2 Icelandic

You don't need to spend money to start preparing for the ríkisborgarapróf. Iceland offers an unusually good selection of free language learning resources — here are the ones worth your time.

Structured courses

Icelandic Online (icelandiconline.com)

The most comprehensive free Icelandic course available. Developed by the University of Iceland, it covers levels from beginner to intermediate with interactive exercises, grammar explanations, vocabulary, and listening practice. It's available in English and several other languages. This alone can take you to A2 level if you work through it consistently.

Grunnnám í íslensku (240-hour basic programme)

This is the subsidised Icelandic programme offered at adult education centres (símenntunarstöðvar) throughout Iceland. While not entirely free, the cost is heavily reduced and some municipalities cover the full fee. The programme is specifically designed to bring immigrants to CEFR A2 — the exact level of the ríkisborgarapróf. Available in Reykjavík (at Mímir and other centres), Akureyri, Ísafjörður, Egilsstaðir, and other towns.

Tungumálatorg (tungumálatorg.is)

A free online platform with exercises organised by topic and skill level. Good for supplementary vocabulary practice and reading comprehension. Less structured than Icelandic Online, but useful for filling gaps.

Listening practice

RÚV — Icelandic National Broadcasting

RÚV offers free streaming of television and radio at ruv.is. For listening practice:

  • RÚV Fréttir — news broadcasts. Start with the main evening news, which uses clear, formal Icelandic.
  • Kastljós — a current affairs programme with interviews and discussions.
  • Children's programmes — often use simpler vocabulary and slower speech, making them ideal for A2 learners.

Podcasts for Icelandic learners

Several podcasts are aimed at Icelandic learners, including "Icelandic for Beginners" and community-produced shows. Search your podcast app for "learn Icelandic" and experiment with different shows until you find one at your level.

Speaking practice

Tungumálakaffi (language cafés)

Free, informal conversation sessions held at libraries and community centres across Iceland. No registration needed — just show up and practise speaking Icelandic with other learners and native speakers. They're held regularly in Reykjavík (Borgarbókasafn), Akureyri, Kópavogur, and other municipalities. Check your local library's website for schedules.

Language exchange partners

Many Icelanders want to practise English or other languages. Offer a language swap — 30 minutes of Icelandic, 30 minutes of your language. Facebook groups like "Language Exchange Iceland" can help you find a partner.

Reading practice

  • Vísir.is and mbl.is — Icelandic news sites. Start with headlines and short articles.
  • Public signs and notices — bus schedules, supermarket signs, official letters. Real-world text at a manageable level.
  • Auðlesið (easy-read) books — several Icelandic publishers produce simplified books for learners and people with reading difficulties. Available at libraries.

Vocabulary tools

  • Stofnun Árna Magnússonar (BÍN) — the official Icelandic word database at bin.arnastofnun.is. Look up any word and see all its forms (cases, tenses, etc.).
  • Anki / Memrise — flashcard apps with community-created Icelandic decks. The spaced repetition system is extremely effective for vocabulary retention.
The most important resource is consistency. 20 minutes of daily practice using any of these tools will get you further than hours of sporadic study. Start today — the ríkisborgarapróf rewards those who prepare steadily over time.
